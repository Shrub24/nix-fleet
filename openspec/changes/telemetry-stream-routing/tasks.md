# Tasks

## 1. Contract and route model

- [x] 1.1 Define named telemetry stream/route declarations and per-route destination selection in the host-local contract; verify defaults preserve the existing general route and configuring an AI backend alone does not route traces to it. `services.telemetry.routes.<route>` carries route-local `signals`/`pipelines` and its own `ingress`; the fixture leaf `telemetry-routes` composes a `langfuse` destination the general route does not name and asserts the general pipeline still exports through `otlphttp/general` alone.
- [x] 1.2 Define how producers or ingress select a route without using span names/resource attributes as implicit classification; verify existing general producer declarations remain valid without route changes. Selection is the listener a producer sends to: the loopback producer listener and `otlp.ingress` remain the general route, a route's own `ingress` carries its traffic, and no attribute-based classification exists in the contract or the realization. The pre-existing general telemetry checks and the ingress runtime check pass unchanged.
- [x] 1.3 Add named validation for unknown routes, unknown destinations, incompatible destination protocols, and ambiguous/default route behavior; verify each invalid case fails with a route-specific error. Named route errors cover an unknown destination in a route pipeline, a route declaring no signals, a signal whose route-local pipeline is empty, an empty or wildcard ingress host, a transportless ingress, a wildcard-or-empty ingress with no admitted signals, and two routes (or a route and the loopback listener) on one socket; the `telemetry-routes` leaf exercises four of them by their message text. A producer cannot name an unknown route because selection is by listener, so an unreachable route is not a representable mistake.

## 2. Gateway realization

- [x] 2.1 Implement independent general and AI-session trace pipelines from their explicit route policies; verify general traffic reaches only general destinations and AI traffic reaches exactly its selected destinations. Each route gets its own receiver (`otlp/route-<route>`), pipeline (`<signal>/route-<route>`) and exporter instance (`<protocol>/route-<route>-<destination>`) with its own file_storage queue and WAL directory; the `telemetry-routes` leaf asserts the general pipeline exports through the general exporter and the `ai` route pipeline, fed by `otlp/route-ai`, exports through `otlphttp/route-ai-langfuse` only.
- [x] 2.2 Preserve route boundaries through processors and batches; add an interleaved mixed-route integration test and verify no record appears at a destination outside its route policy. `tests/telemetry/route_check.py` runs the pinned collector against two mock backends with a 1 s pipeline batch processor: back-to-back general and route traces posted inside one batch window each appear only at their own backend, and every phase asserts absence at the other backend through a bounded settle window rather than leaving it unchecked.
- [x] 2.3 Preserve route-and-destination queue/retry isolation; simulate one AI backend outage with a healthy general destination and verify retries do not leak or replay general-route traces to AI backends. The first harness phase keeps the AI backend down, accepts and queues a route trace, shows the general route still delivering, then delivers the queued route trace on recovery while the general trace stays absent from the recovered backend — so neither route's queued work crosses to the other.
- [x] 2.4 Verify that a complete AI agent/tool/LLM session remains together through delivery and that optional general-store fan-out occurs only when selected by the AI route. The harness sends one three-span resource through the route and observes all three spans at the route backend with none at the general one; the fixture leaf separately asserts the general pipeline never names the AI destination and the route pipeline names only its own selection, so fan-out to the general store is a route-local selection in every case.
